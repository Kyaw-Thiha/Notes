# Hypothesis Test
Suppose we are interested in $\psi(\theta)$. In [[Estimator|point and interval estimate]], we try to guess the value of $\psi(\theta)$ based on the sample observations.

In [[Hypothesis Test|hypothesis test]], we start with a hypothetical statement like $\psi(\theta) = \psi_{0}$. We can call this a [[Hypothesis Test|null hypothesis]] $H_{0}$. The idea is to check whether our observed data supports $H_{0}$ or not.

---
## Null vs Alternative Hypothesis
### Null Hypothsis
The [[#Null Hypothsis|null hypothesis]] $H_{0}$ is the hypothesis we want to test.

### Alternative Hypothesis
The [[#Alternative Hypothesis|alternative hypothesis]] $H_{\alpha}$ is the alternative values of the parameter of interest.

For example, for a null hypothesis of $\mu = 35000$,
- $H_{\alpha}: \mu > 35,000$ or
- $H_{\alpha}: \mu < 35,000$ or
- $H_{\alpha}: \mu \neq 35,000$ or simply,
- $H_{\alpha}: \mu = 45,000$ 

---
## Simple vs Composite Hypothesis
### Simple Hypothesis
[[#Simple Hypothesis|Simple hypothesis]] is when a hypothesis involves a single value from the parameter space.
Eg: $\mu = 35,000$

### Composite Hypothesis
[[#Composite Hypothesis|Composite hypothesis]] is when a hypothesis involves more than one values from the parameter space. Eg: $\mu > 35, 000$ or $\mu \neq 35,000$.
