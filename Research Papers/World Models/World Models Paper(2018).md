## Main Idea
> Our brain learn an abstract representation of both spatial and temporal aspects of the world around us, and develop a mental model.

![500](https://worldmodels.github.io/assets/kitaoka.jpeg)

> **Present is governed by future prediction**
> There is evidence (as shown in patterns above) that what we perceive at any given moment is governed by our brain's prediction of the future based on internal model.

> Predictive model in our brain isn't just predicting the future in general, but rather predicting the future sensory data, given our current motor actions.
> 
> **Eg**: This is why you can't tickle yourself.

---
### Example
> In baseball, a batter has milliseconds to decide how they should swing the bat. 
> Note that this is shorter than time it takes for visual signals to reach the brain.

![300](https://worldmodels.github.io/assets/mccloud_baseball.jpeg)

> The reason they are able to hit a $100\text{ mph}$ fast ball is due to their ability to instinctively predict when and where the ball will go.

> **Against Explicit Prediction**
> They can quickly act on their predictions of the future, without needing to consciously roll out future scenarios to form a plan.

## RL Problem
[[Intro to RL (Chapter-1)|Model-free RL methods]] usually only use small [[Neural Network|neural networks]] with few parameters. 
This is because of the [[Credit Assignment Problem(CAP)|credit assignment problem]].

We can divide the agent into 
- a large world model
- smaller controller model to learn to perform a task using the world model

> Most [[World Models Paper(2018)|model-based RL]] learn a model of RL environment but still trains on actual env.
> 
> The authors explored training only on environment generated by its own internal model. To prevent agent exploiting imperfections in generated environment, temperatur parameter is adjusted to train the agent in noisier, more uncertain environments.

---
### Different Ways of using RL
> **Auxiliary Learning Signal**
> Agent learns to predict future observations, reconstruct inputs, and model the environment as an additional training objective.

> **Model-Based Policy Learning**
> Agent uses its learned model to predict outcomes, plan actions, and generate additional training experience.

> **Learned Model as Generative Environment**
> Use the world model as the controller's training environment. This is what this paper explored using.

## Agent Model
The agent consists of three main components:
- [[#Vision Model|Visual sensory component]] to compress what it sees into small representation.
- [[#Memory Model|Memory component]] that makes predictions about future codes based on past information.
- [[#Controller Model|Decision-making components]] that decides on what actions to take be only on representations by the visual, and memory models.

### Vision Model
The vision model is trained with a [[Variational AutoEncoders (VAE)|VAE]], whose latent space $z$ acts as internal representation for the memory and controller models.

![500](https://worldmodels.github.io/assets/vae.svg)

---
### Memory Model
The [[#Memory Model|memory model]] acts as predictive model of the future $z$ vectors that $V$ is expected to produce.

![400](https://worldmodels.github.io/assets/mdn_rnn_new.svg)


It is made of `MDN-RNN`, which is [[Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)|RNN]] combined with `Mixture Density Network` head.
The `MDN` combines several Gaussians as
$$
p(z_{t+1} \mid a_{t}, z_{t}, h_{t}) = \sum^{K}_{k=1} \pi_{k} \ \mathcal{N}(z_{t+1}; \ \mu_{k}, \sigma^{2}_{k})
$$
During inference, $\sigma'_{k} = \tau\sigma_{k}$ where $\tau$ is the temperature is used to control uncertainty.

---
### Controller Model
The [[#Controller Model|controller model]] is deliberately made as simple as possible:
$$
a_{t} = W_{c} \ [z_{t} \quad h_{t}] + b_{c}
$$
as a [[Neural Network|single linear layer]] mapping concatentated input vector $[z_{t} \quad h_{t}]$ with weight $W_{c}$ and bias $b_{c}$.
